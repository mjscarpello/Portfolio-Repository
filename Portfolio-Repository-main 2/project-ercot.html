DSC 450 / PROJECT 02 / TIME SERIES

Forecasting electricity
demand in ERCOT.

How accurately can historical demand and calendar patterns forecast hourly electricity demand one day ahead, and how should those predictions be compared with an operator's forecast?

Role Data Visualizer & PresenterTeam DSC 450 · Group 1Period January 2022–September 2026

THE REAL-WORLD DECISION

Forecast the hours that matter.

Electricity grid planners must anticipate how much demand customers will place on the system, and when it will peak. In this project, we examined historical ERCOT demand from the U.S. Energy Information Administration's EIA-930 dataset and worked to compare forecasting approaches against reference predictions.

My responsibility as Data Visualizer and Presenter is to make the patterns, comparisons, and implications of the models understandable. That includes asking which errors occur during high-demand periods and whether a reported comparison reflects the same prediction task for every model.

QUESTIONS THAT SHAPED THE VISUALS

Understand first. Compare second.

01 / HISTORICAL DEMAND

Season and time of day

Examine how electricity use changes across the calendar, including warmer months and within-day peaks. The goal is to explain useful model inputs, not just show an attractive heatmap.

02 / FORECAST DESIGN

Reference models

Compare repeat-last-week and reported operator forecasts with model families such as SARIMA/SARIMAX and XGBoost, while keeping track of their prediction horizons and information cutoffs.

03 / WEATHER CONTEXT

Temperature changes the story

Investigate whether historical temperature and calendar signals improve retrospective model comparisons. Weather observed after a forecast is issued cannot automatically be assumed available to a true day-ahead model.

04 / COMMUNICATION

Explain the trade-offs

Present model performance alongside peak-demand behavior and practical limitations. A low aggregate error alone does not demonstrate that a model would be operationally reliable.

PROJECT FIGURES

Real charts, not substitutes.

This area is ready for high-resolution exports from the team's executed notebook. Figures will be included only after we have the original files and their verified captions.

WHAT THE WORK IS TEACHING ME

Not every good-looking forecast is a usable forecast.

The team investigated both static and rolling forecasts. One important modeling lesson was that a long, static SARIMA projection can behave differently from shorter rolling 24-hour forecasts as new observations become available.

Equally important, a fair claim about one-day-ahead performance requires checking exactly what input information was available when each prediction would have been issued. Until the original chart exports and final validation are incorporated, this page intentionally does not publish a definitive model leaderboard or numeric result.
