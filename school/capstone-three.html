01 / THE QUESTION

Why do districts differ?

Citywide averages can conceal local conditions. We examined geographic differences, community characteristics, persistently low-share districts and forecasting.

What we measure: dry recycling collection share, not verified material recovery, individual recycling behavior or a measure of total diversion.

TARGET DEFINITION

Use summed tonnage

Dry share (%) = 100 × (Paper + MGP) / (Refuse + Paper + MGP)

Annual shares are calculated from summed annual tonnage, rather than averaging monthly percentages. MGP means metal, glass and plastic.

GEOGRAPHIC INTEGRITY

59 districts ≠ 55 sub-borough areas

Income data aligns to 59 district codes, but community indicators can use differing geographic proxies and reporting periods. Boundary and uncertainty limitations must remain visible rather than being hidden in the join.

What this demonstrates: meaningful analysis starts before modeling. Consistent definitions, documented decisions and transparent limitations give collaborators a foundation they can trust.

04 / INTERPRETATION

The 2025 collection shift.

Forecast errors reveal why understanding the underlying collection process matters. Actual collection share was about one percentage point above forecasts beginning in April 2025.

+158%Compost collection
−3.7%Refuse collection
+3–4%Paper and MGP collection

April–October 2025 versus the same months in 2024. Reduced refuse alone accounts for about half of the one-point increase in the collection-share measure. These changes do not establish that more material was ultimately recovered.

Use results to guide investigation and outreach. Avoid assigning blame or attributing district-level associations to individual residents.
