The finding

Why overall accuracy hides the peak-demand pattern.

01 / THE FINDING

Average accuracy doesn't tell the whole story.

A forecast can perform well overall but make very different errors during the hours that place the most demand on the grid.

Our team studied next-day hourly demand in the ERCOT region. Sarah prepared the data, Andrea developed the forecast approaches, and I examined model predictions and errors through original visual analysis.

The differing error patterns motivated our experiment with a fixed 50/50 blend of Rolling SARIMA and Weather XGBoost.

THE CENTRAL RESULT

The hybrid led our team models overall. During the highest observed demand hours, Rolling SARIMA and Weather SARIMAX were much more accurate.

Research scopeThis retrospective study evaluates 8,640 matched hourly records from October 2025 to September 2026 using historical EIA-930 demand and the team's forecasts. It examines overall and high-demand accuracy; forecast issuance conditions and weather information were not identical across all approaches.

02 / THE INVESTIGATION

How visualizing errors changed our approach.

Four steps show how I moved from comparing model performance to evaluating an ensemble, then questioning its limits.

01 / ESTABLISH THE BENCHMARK

First, compare each forecast.

Before exploring improvements, I compared the team's models with ERCOT's published day-ahead forecast. Weather XGBoost led the individual team approaches on the common sample, but the operator benchmark still had the lowest percentage error.

The models weren't making identical mistakes.

Visualizing signed errors from Rolling SARIMA and Weather XGBoost exposed differences that an overall accuracy ranking could not show. Those differences led me to investigate whether their forecasts could complement each other.

03 / TEST AN ENSEMBLE

Could a simple blend perform better?

The team tested a fixed 50/50 average of Rolling SARIMA and Weather XGBoost using the existing predictions. The hybrid reached 2,006 MWh overall MAE on the matched sample, the lowest among our approaches. We also evaluated blends over a later period, shown in the accompanying F22 figure.

Improved overall team accuracy; ERCOT remained the stronger benchmark at 1,135 MWh MAE.

Open original F22 · Separate later-period blend evaluation ↗
04 / CHALLENGE THE RESULT

Winning overall doesn't mean winning at peak demand.

When we examined the highest observed demand hours, the ranking changed. Rolling SARIMA and Weather SARIMAX were much more accurate than the tree-based approaches. The hybrid's overall advantage did not translate into being the best option at every demand level.

THE RESEARCH BOUNDARY

What these results do—and do not—show.

This is a retrospective study, not an operational forecasting deployment. Models do not necessarily use identical issue times or information available before the forecast hour. Some weather-enhanced experiments use observed historical temperatures, not advance weather predictions. The highest-demand 1% is identified using actual demand after the fact. Neither matched error scores nor a research graph prove causality or quantify prevented outages or financial savings.
